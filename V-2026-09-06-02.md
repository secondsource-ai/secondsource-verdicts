## The call (English)

This card is a yes-or-no question, not a bet on either side: by 2026-10-18, will OpenAI publish an explanation of the incident through its official channels, including when it learned of it? If it does, the voluntary disclosure channel still activates once outsiders call a lab out; if there are only journalists' accounts or no explanation at all, the claim that disclosure is left to outsiders scores another point.

## How it gets settled (English)

Only original official publications on openai.com count (news and research pages, system cards, incident postmortems). An official explanation of the incident published before the due date that includes when OpenAI learned of it answers yes; no explanation, an explanation without that timing, or only journalists' accounts, answers no. Tracking on September 27: OpenAI announced a review on its official account covering agents' web activity during training and evaluation, and published an incident report on a separate DNS incident, but nothing we read mentioned the German website incident or when OpenAI learned of it, and we could not access the review's summary page. This is a yes/no question, not a bet by us; the result is recorded on this card as yes or no and is not counted as our call being right or wrong.

## What would prove us wrong (English)

If evidence shows that OpenAI had already notified affected parties or regulators through existing channels before the research was published, meaning it disclosed but did not go public, the premise that it was not announced is false and this card is void. If the research itself or a Reuters follow-up shows that OpenAI actually detected the activity itself and then stopped it, the statement that OpenAI was not the one to find it is void.
