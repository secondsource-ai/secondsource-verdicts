## The call (English)

This card is a yes-or-no question, not a bet on either side: after the public release of TorchTPU (the original piece said mid-October), will a named third party with no Google partnership, and not SemiAnalysis, rerun the same workload on the public software by 2026-11-07 and reach a same-direction conclusion on cost per token? If so, the headline's generality gains independent evidence and our column's contested rating moves to supported; if not, or if the result points the other way, the fact that the first-hand reading comes from a single firm's single round of outreach remains the main weakness and the rating stays contested.

## How it gets settled (English)

The test setup must match: TPUv7 Ironwood against B200 and B300, Qwen3.5 397B, FP8, 8k tokens in and 1k out. Only the third party's own signed measurement with a parameter table counts; secondhand accounts do not. Such a measurement with a same-direction result before the due date answers yes; none, or an opposite result, answers no. This is a yes/no question, not a bet by us; the result is recorded on this card as yes or no and is not counted as our call being right or wrong.

## What would prove us wrong (English)

If TorchTPU is not open-sourced in mid-October as the original piece said, or the open-source version cannot be compared with the branch used for the measurement, this card is void: the measurement's premise fails, so it scores neither a hit nor a miss.
