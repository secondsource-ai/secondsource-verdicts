## The call (English)

Our existing view is that whether TPUs take share hinges on externalization, not on price-performance. This card is a yes-or-no question, not a bet on either side: by 2026-10-31, will Google sign a verifiable long-term TPU contract with a named outside customer, with capacity or term that can be checked? If so, the externalization switch has started to turn, the load-bearing leg of our view holds, and the next question is whether share follows; if not, the switch has not moved, and however good the price-performance readings are, they are not evidence of a threat to share.

## How it gets settled (English)

Only Google or Alphabet's official press releases and SEC filings, and the customer's own official press releases and statutory filings, count. Any of these naming an outside customer's long-term TPU contract with capacity or term before the due date answers yes; no such announcement or filing answers no. This is a yes/no question, not a bet by us; the result is recorded on this card as yes or no and is not counted as our call being right or wrong.

## What would prove us wrong (English)

If verifiable readings of share movement (third-party market measurements) appear while no outside long-term contract can be found, attributing the bottleneck to externalization is wrong and the result has to be recorded as driven by price-performance.
